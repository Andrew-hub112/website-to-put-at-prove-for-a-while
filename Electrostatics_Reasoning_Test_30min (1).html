1. Opposite charges and charge density

Charges +q and −q are at distinct positions r1 and r2. What follows from their zero total charge?

ρ(r) = qδ³(r−r₁) − qδ³(r−r₂)
Zero total charge is a global statement. Delta distributions at distinct points do not cancel locally; the configuration is a dipole and generally produces a nonzero field.

2. What a measurement over a subvolume detects

For ρ(r)=3qδ³(r−r₁)−2qδ³(r−r₂), a volume V contains r₂ but not r₁. What is ∫Vρ dτ?

The selection property includes only delta distributions whose support lies inside V; therefore the enclosed charge is −2q.

3. Potential of a uniformly charged disk

A uniform disk lies in the xy-plane. At an arbitrary observation point, which conclusion is most accurate?

Axial symmetry removes dependence on the azimuthal angle, not on the cylindrical radius s. Off-axis evaluation generally involves elliptic integrals.

4. Potential versus field at a charged surface

For an ideal disk with nonzero surface density σ, what happens when one crosses the disk away from its edge?

A finite surface charge does not make the potential discontinuous. The boundary condition is Eₙ⁺−Eₙ⁻=σ/ε₀.

5. Green function and pair counting

Why does the continuous electrostatic energy contain the factor 1/2?

U = ½ ∬ ρ(r)G(r,r′)ρ(r′) dτ dτ′
The ordered contributions (r,r′) and (r′,r) represent the same physical pair, so the factor 1/2 removes double counting.

6. Point charges and self-energy

When ρ is replaced by a sum of Dirac deltas in the Green-function energy, why are terms with i=j excluded?

The Coulomb Green function behaves as 1/|r−r′| and diverges at coincident points. The finite interaction energy retains only i≠j terms.

7. What “closed boundary” means

A Gaussian cylinder has a lateral surface and two circular caps. Which flux must appear in Gauss’s law?

The boundary ∂V is the union of every surface piece enclosing V. Symmetry may make some contributions zero, but they still belong to the closed-boundary integral.

8. External charges and net flux

A charge outside a closed surface produces a nonzero electric field on that surface. What is its contribution to the net flux?

External charges affect E point by point, but their total flux through a closed boundary is zero because no source from them lies within V.

9. Divergence theorem as the bridge

Which chain correctly converts local Gauss’s law into its integral form?

The divergence theorem converts outward flux through ∂V into the volume integral of ∇·E; Maxwell’s equation then replaces ∇·E with ρ/ε₀.

10. Zero enclosed charge: the subtle conclusion

A closed surface encloses equal positive and negative charges. Which conclusion necessarily follows?

Gauss’s law fixes the net flux, not the field at each surface point. Positive and negative flux contributions can cancel.
